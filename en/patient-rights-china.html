Patient Rights During Treatment in China

Medical treatment abroad involves more than choosing a hospital and a doctor. Patients should understand what information they receive before a medical intervention, what documents they sign, how their medical information is handled and which records they should obtain before returning home.

Practical Patient Pathway

Before Travel, During Treatment and Before Returning Home

Patient interests are easier to protect when important documents, payments and arrangements are recorded throughout the medical journey rather than only after a problem occurs.

BEFORE TRAVEL

Check the pathway

  • which healthcare institution is reviewing the case;
  • which medical documents are being submitted;
  • whether medical translation is required;
  • what is included in the coordination service;
  • which costs relate to medical care and which relate to travel or coordination;
  • whether additional examinations may be required.
WHILE IN CHINA

Keep medical information and records

  • ask the doctor questions about the proposed treatment;
  • request clarification before signing documents you do not understand;
  • keep examination and test results;
  • keep evidence of payments;
  • use language support when necessary;
  • distinguish medical decisions from the coordinator's organisational role.
BEFORE RETURNING HOME

Collect your documents

  • a discharge summary or other final medical document, where available;
  • laboratory and diagnostic results;
  • available medical imaging materials;
  • information about procedures and treatment provided;
  • prescriptions and recommendations;
  • records needed for continued care in Kazakhstan.
Key Principles

What Matters Most for an International Patient

The exact procedure for exercising patient rights may depend on the healthcare institution, the type of medical intervention and the applicable rules in China. The points below are practical considerations for patients travelling from Kazakhstan.

01

Informed Consent

For surgery, special examinations or treatment, the healthcare side must follow applicable requirements concerning patient information and consent. If you do not understand a document, request an explanation before signing it.

02

Respect and Privacy

Medical information is sensitive information. Healthcare institutions and medical personnel are expected to comply with applicable requirements concerning patient privacy and medical records.

03

Medical Records

Patients should clarify in advance how they can obtain available copies of medical records, diagnostic results and final medical documents from the particular healthcare institution.

04

Understanding Costs

Before making a payment, identify who is receiving the money and what the payment covers: medical treatment, diagnostics, translation, transport, accommodation or a separate coordination service.

Medical Records

Do Not Leave Without Checking Your Documents

After returning to Kazakhstan, a patient may need continued treatment, medical monitoring or consultation with another doctor. Medical records obtained in China may therefore be important for continuity of care.

The exact set of documents depends on the individual case and the procedures of the healthcare institution. Before discharge, ask what medical materials can be provided to you and in what format.

✓
Discharge Summary Where provided, a final document describing the hospital stay, the patient's condition and the treatment received.
✓
Diagnostic and Test Results Laboratory results, pathology reports and other available diagnostic materials.
✓
Medical Imaging Ask whether available CT, MRI, PET-CT or other imaging materials can be provided.
✓
Treatment Information Information about surgery, procedures, medications and other treatment details made available by the healthcare institution.
✓
Recommendations Follow-up care, monitoring, further examinations and other recommendations from the treating medical team.
✓
Payment Records Keep invoices, receipts and other evidence of payments made during the medical journey.
Clear Responsibilities

The Healthcare Institution and ChinaMed Have Different Roles

This distinction is particularly important when requesting documents, asking questions or dealing with a complaint or dispute.

Healthcare Institution

  • performs medical diagnostics;
  • determines medical indications;
  • proposes and provides medical treatment;
  • obtains required medical consent;
  • maintains medical records;
  • is responsible for its own medical activities under the applicable rules.

ChinaMed Kazakhstan

  • coordinates the patient's medical journey;
  • helps organise the transfer of medical documents;
  • coordinates communication and translation;
  • assists with agreed organisational aspects of the trip;
  • does not diagnose medical conditions;
  • does not prescribe treatment or guarantee a medical outcome.
Medical Information

Sharing Medical and Personal Data

Organising treatment may require a patient to provide documents containing personal and medical information. There should be a clear purpose for sharing such information, for example medical review by a healthcare institution, arranging a consultation or preparing the patient's journey.

A

Share What Is Necessary

Before sending documents, understand what information is needed for the relevant stage of the medical journey and who will receive it.

B

Consider Cross-Border Data Transfer

When documents are sent from Kazakhstan to China, information is transferred between jurisdictions. The separate ChinaMed Kazakhstan medical and personal data page will explain the relevant data-handling arrangements in more detail.

If a Question Arises

First Determine Who the Issue Should Be Addressed To

Not every issue relates to ChinaMed. A medical issue and an issue concerning the coordination service are different matters.

01 / MEDICAL CARE

Question About Diagnosis or Treatment

Questions concerning diagnosis, surgery, medication, complications, medical indications or the quality of medical treatment should primarily be addressed to the doctor and healthcare institution.

02 / COORDINATION

Question for ChinaMed Kazakhstan

If the issue concerns the agreed coordination service, transfer of documents, translation or another organisational action performed by ChinaMed, the request should be addressed to the coordinator.

Independent Legal Assistance

When a Medical Matter Becomes a Legal Matter

ChinaMed Kazakhstan does not replace a lawyer. If the patient needs an independent legal assessment, legal assistance should remain separate from medical coordination.

Legal assistance may become relevant when reviewing contracts and documents, dealing with payment disputes, determining the rights and obligations of the parties, interacting with a foreign counterparty or preparing a legal position. Seeking legal advice is a separate decision made by the patient.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About Patient Rights in China

Do patients have the right to receive information about their treatment?
China applies the principle of informed consent. In relevant circumstances, the healthcare side is required to explain important medical information concerning the proposed intervention and obtain the necessary consent. The exact procedure depends on the clinical situation and applicable rules.
Can I obtain copies of my medical records?
Chinese rules provide for access to and copying of certain medical record materials. The exact documents available and the procedure for obtaining them should be confirmed with the particular healthcare institution.
Who decides which treatment I receive?
Medical decisions are made by doctors and the healthcare institution based on the patient's clinical situation and the required patient consent. ChinaMed Kazakhstan does not diagnose conditions, prescribe medication or determine a treatment method.
What should I obtain before returning to Kazakhstan?
Consider confirming the availability of a discharge summary or other final medical document, test and diagnostic results, information about treatment received, prescriptions, recommendations and other records needed for continued medical care.
Who should I contact if a problem occurs?
First identify the nature of the issue. Medical matters should be addressed to the healthcare institution. Questions concerning the coordination service should be addressed to ChinaMed Kazakhstan. If independent legal assessment is required, the patient may separately consult a lawyer.
